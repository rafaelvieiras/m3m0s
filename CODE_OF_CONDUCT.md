# Code of Conduct

This project exists to build good software. The rules below are short on
purpose: keep the work technical, keep it civil, and judge contributions by
their merit.

## What we expect

- **Judge the work, not the person.** Issues, pull requests and reviews are
  evaluated on technical merit: correctness, clarity, scope and maintenance cost.
- **Be direct and civil.** Disagree with ideas, not with people. Blunt technical
  criticism is fine; insults are not.
- **Assume good faith** and ask before assuming a mistake was intentional.
- **Stay on topic.** Project spaces are for the project. Campaigning on
  politics, religion or other off-topic causes does not belong in issues, pull
  requests or discussions, whatever the position.
- **Own your mistakes.** If you got something wrong, say so and fix it.

## What is not acceptable

- Threats, or violence-glorifying language.
- Harassment: repeated unwanted contact, or following someone from thread to
  thread to antagonize them.
- Publishing someone's private information (doxxing).
- Personal attacks and insults in place of arguments.
- Spam, advertising, or deliberately disruptive behavior (flooding, trolling).
- Submitting code or content you do not have the right to contribute.

## What this document does not do

- It does **not** exclude, penalize or rank anyone for their identity,
  background, opinions, beliefs, religion, political views or what they say
  elsewhere. Only conduct inside the project's spaces is in scope.
- It does **not** require anyone to adopt, affirm or recite any ideology or
  vocabulary.
- It is not a license to silence legitimate technical disagreement.

## Scope

This code applies to project spaces: the repository, its issues, pull requests,
discussions and any official channel run by the maintainers. Behavior outside
these spaces is not grounds for moderation here, unless it is a direct threat or
targeted harassment of a project participant.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit, hide, lock or remove content that breaks these rules, and
will say why when it is practical to do so. Responses are proportionate: a
reminder first, then a temporary block for repeated or serious cases, and a
permanent block only for threats, doxxing, targeted harassment or persistent
abuse after warnings.

To report a problem or contest a moderation decision, contact the maintainers
privately through the repository's private reporting channel (GitHub Security
Advisories: **Security → Report a vulnerability**) and describe what happened,
with links. Reports are handled in confidence and reviewed on a best-effort
basis. Maintainers who are involved in an incident do not decide on it alone.
